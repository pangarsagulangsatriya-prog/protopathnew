PROTO PATH - WHERE ARE YOU DEPART FROM ? 
TEXT · SPATIAL SCORE 
 
DRAFT  ·  ON DEVELOPMENT  ·  Proto path / Urban Body Score 
 
INTRODUCTORY NOTE 
This document contains the draft text for "Proto Path: Where Are You Depart From?". The work is still in development. To give 
you the full context, Part II: Research Trajectory is included at the end. It tracks our process from object-theatre experiments, 
through the adoption of procedural work systems, to the expansion into urban-spatial observation of Bandung. 
 
METHOD FRAMEWORK  -  context note 
1. DATASET 
The dataset originates from two Mapillary JSON files containing street-level visual data from the city of Bandung. Each entry 
represents a single feature point in the street: an object, traffic sign, marking, or infrastructure element. Data is read as points, 
coordinates, distances, feature types, and raw values. 
Two files are used: mapillary-points (110,299 object points; 39 value types) and mapillary-traffic (3,778 sign points; 91 value 
types). All movement instructions in this score are drawn directly from these two sources. 
2. MFI - MAPILLARY FEATURE INDEX 
In this research, the visual feature index from Mapillary is operationalized as MFI (Mapillary Feature Index): a reading system that 
categorizes objects, signs, markings, construction elements, and other spatial features as performative triggers. Each value within 
the MFI is translated into a specific spatial condition and body action. 
3. DATA AS PERFORMANCE 
Data is not used to represent the city as narrative. It operates as a direct trigger for body action. Data becomes the environmental 
input. The actor's body becomes the output cursor. The stage becomes the translation space between the feature log and the 
performative impulse. 
 
 
 
THE LIVING TEXT 
This score is conceived not as a static blueprint, but as a continuously evolving "living text." To experience this ongoing 
iteration, you are invited to visit  
 
protopathproject.com 
 
where this text is rendered as an interactive data visualization. In that digital space, the score operates as an agnostic 
engine,allowing anyone to replace the current dataset and seamlessly recontextualize the spatial triggers for any other 
city. 
 
Through this alternative encounter, the text proves its extreme flexibility and contextuality. Local audiences or text-makers 
could literally plug their own local street data.whether from Yogyakarta, Tokyo, or Manila,into this dramaturgical engine. 
The presentation becomes an interactive spatial reading or an object-based archive, where the audience witnesses 
firsthand how different datasets generate entirely different kinetic instructions, transforming the room into a laboratory for 
spatial architecture.

CATATAN TENTANG DATA, OBJEK, DAN TUBUH 
In this document, the body does not stand alone as a performative subject. It functions as a response system to the pressure of 
data. Every street object (object, regulatory, warning, construction, marking) is translated into a spatial situation, and every spatial 
situation triggers a specific body impulse. 
Codes such as [LINEAR_OBJECT], [SOLID_MASS_OBJECT], and [REGULATORY_OBJECT] are not stage decoration. They 
are active agents dictating posture, pressure, gaze direction, and duration of hold.

RESEARCH TRAJECTORY 
Data as Performance. Traces of the research process across experiments. 
 
 
This research grew organically, in layers, in stages : a choreographic exploration moving from the microcosm (observation 
of inanimate objects on a tabletop), expanding into industrial work systems, and finally reaching into the macrocosm (the 
city's streets). The entire trajectory settles into one abstraction : spatial data acting as score for the performer's body. 
The red thread across all phases of experimentation holds to one principle : data is not treated as narrative to be 
represented but as direct trigger of action. 
 
 
I.  Object as Text  ·  Diagnosing the Body 
 
 
The experiment began at the Kalampuan workshop with Mawar Jingga. As a scenographer, Mawar wanted to write a 
script using the language of space and material. The hierarchy was inverted : what held authority was the inanimate 
object, while the human body became no more than a cursor obedient to the object's repetition. 
The observation material was highly specific : hospital pain documents and records of domestic Work-Related 
Musculoskeletal Disorders. Because medical records could not be accessed, the diagnostic procedure of the physician 
was restructured into movement instructions. 
This shift produced a body score. Through diagnostic instructions rewritten as stage tasks, the repeated interaction 
between hand and object on the tabletop slowly forced the performer's body to lock itself into a real injury posture. The 
object was the one calibrating the body.
